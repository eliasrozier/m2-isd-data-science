Viterbi: dynamic programming over states and time

The word loop: one HMM made of all word models

Leaving the last state of word u and entering the first state of word v costs λ · log P(v | u) + β. The source state tells us u, so a bigram needs no extra states.

Why the language model needs a weight and the words a penalty

Scale mismatch

log P(X | W) sums ~100 frame terms per second; log P(W) adds one term per word.

Frame terms are not independent: the acoustic score is overconfident.

λ: LM weight

Rebalances prior against evidence.

Too small: acoustics alone. Too large: the LM overrides what was said.

β: insertion penalty

Each word entry costs β: controls insertions vs deletions.

Scale: tens of nats here, not 1.

Protocol

Grid over β for each λ on dev utterances.

Report WER on separate test utterances.

Check the optimum is not on the grid edge (it was, at first).

A confound to know

λ also scales the uniform LM: a large λ alone acts as an insertion penalty.

Compare LMs at a FIXED λ to isolate what the LM content brings.

Beam pruning trades accuracy for speed

Rule

At each frame keep only states within B of the best score.

Effect

Search errors appear when the right path looks bad early.

Cost becomes roughly proportional to active states.

Four traps we fell into while building this lab

Silence

Untrimmed recordings: the word 'six' absorbed every pause. All errors were insertions.

Penalty scale

β = ±4 changed nothing: the acoustic scores live in tens of nats.

Normalisation

Isolated words and long utterances had different feature statistics.

Grid edge

The best β sat on the edge of the grid: the LM gain was partly a penalty gain.
